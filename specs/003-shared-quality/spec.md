## User Scenarios & Testing

Visitors and signed-in users view sporting data; authorized users perform actions within their domain. The operator receives alerts and conducts recovery according to their permissions and availability. Tools and providers are implementation means, not additional business roles.

The scenarios define required outcomes; actual implementation and qualification are tracked in [tasks](tasks.md) and [quickstart](quickstart.md). Identifiers are local to this specification. A scenario does not establish production availability or restoration evidence.

### User Story 1 — Consult correct, current authorised data (Priority: P1)

Users consult sporting details, calendars and search with accepted changes reflected
in the relevant views, without restrictions being bypassed or supplier failure being
presented as a sporting deletion.

**Why this priority**: Correct content and understandable failures are required for
ordinary use; no workload, latency benchmark or capacity qualification is required.

**Independent Test**: Accept a correction, consult/refresh its related views,
then exercise a supplier failure and a visibility restriction.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A01** — **Given** a delivery candidate, **when** its qualification is prepared, **then** functional correctness and domain timing are checked without a synthetic workload, latency or capacity programme. (FR-001–FR-003)
2. **A02** — **Given** an accepted sporting correction, **when** affected views are consulted/refreshed and derived projection processing completes, **then** they use the accepted state rather than restoring an older version. Current visibility and permissions apply even before a projection catches up; no continuous screen refresh or global propagation stopwatch is required. (FR-004, FR-005)
3. **A03** — **Given** an unavailable supplier and retained consultable data, **when** a user opens its details or calendar, **then** the accepted data remains usable under F02 with honest freshness. The supplier failure is visible to operations, not interpreted as sporting absence or successful integration. (FR-005, FR-008)

### User Story 2 — Understand incidents and intervene without mandatory on-call duty (Priority: P1)

As an independent operator, I want actionable alerts and the information needed to intervene when I am available.

**Why this priority**: Actionable incidents support manual intervention; unavailable supervision does not establish healthy service.

**Independent Test**: Exercise bounded incidents and inspect alerts, diagnostics and practical recovery instructions; no monthly availability calculation.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A04** — **Given** operational monitoring, **when** its scope is reviewed, **then** it covers incident detection under A05–A07 without monthly availability calculation or reporting. (FR-006, FR-007)
2. **A05** — **Given** significant unavailability, including loss of the production host or its local monitoring, **when** it is detected, **then** an alert can reach the operator without depending on that host functioning. Detection time, scope and last known state are available. The operator intervenes according to availability; no guaranteed intervention time is announced. (FR-009–FR-011)
3. **A06** — **Given** a persistent F01 acquisition failure or a later pause, **when** native alert evaluation repeats or closes its condition, **then** reports remain grouped by useful scope with five-day reminders and the native closure is not treated as evidence of business recovery. The operator checks a usable observation of the affected scope; acquisition success does not prove a failed integration recovered. No separate incident ledger is required. (FR-010, FR-012)
4. **A07** — **Given** partial integration or a refused retry, **when** the operator views diagnostics and instructions, **then** they distinguish successful elements, failed elements, affected scope, reason and authorized recovery action. Useful information exposes no secret, personal content or raw provider document. Only the scoped original-target references for private F05 manual cleanup are permitted under FR-013; no such reference reaches a public notification. (FR-010, FR-013, FR-014)

### User Story 3 — Preserve rights and action effects (Priority: P1)

As a user, I want failures and retries neither to give another account access to my data nor to allow unauthorized actions, and not to duplicate an already accepted action.

**Why this priority**: Cached information never replaces current authorisation decisions.

**Independent Test**: Submit authorized and prohibited requests, change accounts during a deferred response, then replay an operation whose confirmation was lost.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A08** — **Given** a visitor, an account without the required right or a request concerning another account’s data, **when** a protected action is requested directly or from an old interface, **then** it is refused without a prohibited effect. Absent or unusable entitlement evidence grants no implicit right; still-valid evidence and tolerances explicitly defined by F05/F09 remain applicable. (FR-015, FR-017, FR-018)
2. **A09** — **Given** a still-pending response intended for account A, **when** the user signs out or uses account B, **then** this response does not populate session B and account A’s private data or rights do not remain usable by B. (FR-016, FR-018)
3. **A10** — **Given** an ordinary accepted action whose response was lost, **when** its result is reread or a safe authorized retry occurs, **then** ordinary duplicate effects are prevented and uncertainty is not called cancellation. Apply domain-specific exceptional delivery limits, including manual resolution of a rare duplicate F10 issue. No generic recovery protocol is an acceptance condition. (FR-008, FR-019)

### User Story 4 — Recover complete state after interrupted processing (Priority: P1)

As an operator, I want to rerun processing or rebuild derived data without losing correct results or bringing obsolete state back into use.

**Why this priority**: Partially rebuilt search must not be presented as a complete empty result.

**Independent Test**: Interrupt integration and rebuilding at several stages, replay observations and compare retained data, identities and final visibility.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A11** — **Given** accepted partial results and a visible failure, **when** the next normal collection, standard rerun or authorized manual correction occurs, **then** accepted data and identities are preserved, current restrictions apply and completion is reported honestly. Resuming the exact interrupted step is not required. (FR-019, FR-021)
2. **A12** — **Given** usable search and an interrupted rebuild, **when** derived data has been partially prepared, **then** it does not replace usable results with an empty or incomplete state presented as complete. Currently hidden or prohibited data is not re-exposed through retained results. Without usable prior state, unavailability is explicit rather than a false “no results”. (FR-020)
3. **A13** — **Given** an old observation or work interrupted before a division restriction, **when** it is replayed, **then** it does not overwrite newer accepted state or bypass current restrictions. Rebuilding alone does not re-emit processed announcements; F07 accepts an exceptional duplicate if restoration lost match-level facts. (FR-019–FR-021)

### User Story 5 — Restore retained data and files (Priority: P1)

As an operator, I want actually restorable backups, with an explicit recovered scope and files consistent with the restored data.

**Why this priority**: The presence of a database backup or file URL does not demonstrate recovery of the whole set.

**Independent Test**: Restore copies in an isolated environment, making original storage unavailable, and check actual recovered scope, files, rights and applicable obligations using standard tools and manual review.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A14** — **Given** scheduled independent backups with an initial 30-minute interval, **when** production storage is lost and a usable copy is restored manually, **then** user/operator data and required files are checked against that copy and any loss is explicit. Acquired data has no separate four-hour loss bound; new collection reconstructs only what sources still publish. An old logo URL alone does not preserve its bytes. (FR-022–FR-024)
2. **A15** — **Given** scheduled data and image backups, **when** a job fails or the schedule stops, **then** no backup notification, dashboard or automatic verification is promised. When in doubt, the operator can inspect existing job history and destination copies manually; a started job or present file does not prove restoration. Normal retention still expires old copies. (FR-023, FR-025)
3. **A16** — **Given** a copy independent of the production host, **when** the single initial manual restoration exercise runs in isolation before relying on V2 protection, **then** expected data, files and associations are recovered, derivatives can be rebuilt and gaps are identified. Record actual duration and limits without a recurring drill or intervention-time guarantee. (FR-024, FR-026)
4. **A17** — **Given** a backup predating applicable erasure, rights changes or notification purge, **when** restoring it manually, **then** the operator checks and applies current obligations before affected access or sends resume. Missing evidence leaves affected capabilities unavailable. No automatic reconstruction of all interrupted operations is required. (FR-027; F05/F09/F11 and F07 A51)
5. **A18** — **Given** a failing application version, **when** returning to a previous version is considered, **then** its compatibility with retained data is checked. An image rollback claims neither to undo a data change nor to replace restoration; incompatibility prevents this unsafe rollback and directs the operator to the documented recovery procedure. After V2 opens, F14 requires its recovery without requiring a functional return to V1. (FR-014, FR-028)

As an operator who has associated club logos, I want the authorized reset not to destroy this manual work.

**Why this priority**: Files are retained in S3, but their club association resides in the V1 database; losing that association may make the files unusable.

**Independent Test**: Examine a controlled recovery set with an identified club, an ambiguous association and a missing file, according to F02 rules and the transition prerequisites handed to F14.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A22** — **Given** associations between V1 club references and logo URLs, **when** their protection and recovery are verified for F14, **then** each expected association and file is retained or explicitly flagged as missing. ### Edge Cases

- Traffic anecdotes are historical context, not workload requirements or capacity evidence.
- A fast error, hidden data served from a cache or obsolete information presented as fresh does not satisfy a quality objective.
- Timely collection, successful integration and propagation to views remain three distinct observations.
- A monitoring gap is not evidence of availability; an external incident and a Blockout outage are not confused when they have different effects on journeys.
- A backup stored independently of the production host may still be incomplete or unusable; file deletion may invalidate an older database copy.
- Restoration may recover rights or data that have become prohibited since the copy. Current restrictions must precede making them available again.
- An old club absent from rebuilt competitions retains its logo association in recovery material; that association alone does not create a new viewable club.

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

#### Functional quality and freshness

- **FR-001**: No concurrency or action-rate target is retained in the current scope or deferred roadmap.
- **FR-002**: No load-test, benchmark or capacity-study protocol is required for this scope or release.
- **FR-003**: No numerical API latency or percentile acceptance target is retained. Ordinary correctness and bounded user waits remain required by FR-008.
- **FR-004**: Accepted sporting changes MUST update their affected views and derived projections. New consultation/refresh MUST respect current permissions and visibility; a stale projection never authorises disclosure. Keep freshness honest and update failed projections through normal processing/manual correction. No global numerical propagation objective or continuous screen refresh is required.
- **FR-005**: Distinguish supplier observation, acquisition, accepted integration and completed projection. Success at one step MUST NOT prove the next; failed or pending integration/projection remains diagnosable. F01 collection timing and domain-owned expiry/freshness rules remain applicable without a global performance qualification.

#### Incident detection and operations

- **FR-006**: No monthly availability target or accounting is required. Incident detection and safe operations remain under FR-008–FR-014.
- **FR-007**: No monthly availability report, coverage calculation or retained proof series is required. Missing diagnostics MUST NOT be represented as healthy operation under FR-010.
- **FR-008**: Isolate failures to the capabilities that actually depend on them. Retained viewable data MUST remain usable without claiming a failed refresh succeeded. Keep ordinary loading, empty, failure and retry outcomes understandable. Internal distinctions do not require separate screens: F06 follows and F07 reads reconcile discreetly, F10 support permits explicit retry after an ordinary send error without a receipt lookup, and F03 PDF opening uses an ordinary error. Preserve domain-specific safeguards for purchases, erasure and moderation. Bound waits and attempts; never invent success or block navigation indefinitely.
- **FR-009**: Important application incidents MUST automatically alert the operator: essential-journey failure, failed integration/reconstruction or projection, and F01 acquisition incidents. Backup failure and scheduling are explicitly excluded from this alert obligation under FR-025. F05 external-cleanup failures alert from observed private logs; a process interruption after local commit but before logging may remain undetected, without a durable deletion ledger. Host-loss detection and application-alert transmission MUST NOT depend only on the monitored production host. Document and verify the selected application monitoring; no monthly availability accounting is required.
- **FR-010**: The operator MUST be able to know the affected scope, time and outcome of the last observation, last success, pending or failed work and available partial-success counts. Never run, awaiting eligibility, paused, in progress, completed and failed states MUST remain distinguishable. Inaccessible monitoring or execution without a confirmed outcome MUST NOT be presented as healthy. F05 cleanup diagnostics do not guarantee an exhaustive record of obligations across process interruption.
- **FR-011**: Recovery from a significant incident MUST allow authorized manual intervention according to operator availability. No guaranteed human deadline or permanent on-call duty is required. Detection, alerting, start of intervention and recovery MUST remain distinct in operational information to measure durations without hiding the wait for human intervention.
- **FR-012**: Application problem notifications MUST use native grouping by meaningful problem and scope, with reminders every five days and native closure notifications. Closure of an alert condition, including after pause, configuration change or missing series, MUST NOT by itself be represented as confirmed business recovery. Inspect affected-scope execution outcomes and positive observations for that conclusion; success elsewhere or acquisition success alone does not prove integration recovered. Preserve F01 detection thresholds and distinguish missing observations from healthy execution. No independent incident ledger, exact logical-transition notification guarantee or custom closure engine is required; native delivery limitations remain explicit. Backup notifications are excluded under FR-025.
- **FR-013**: Diagnostics MUST link failure stages and scope through technical references, times, outcome categories and safe actionable reasons. Exclude secrets, tokens, personal content, attachments and raw provider documents. Only the original account/provider references and minimum verification evidence needed for F05 manual cleanup may appear in private logs under F11 FR-027, with the existing 14-day retention. No personal target reference appears in Discord alerts, public artifacts or user errors. User-facing errors offer a usable next step without internal details. No retention duration is inferred from the 30 backup days.
- **FR-014**: The operator MUST have concise usable instructions for relevant standard operations: interpreting alerts, checking backups, manually resolving important incomplete actions, rebuilding derived data, restoring and evaluating a compatible rollback. State access prerequisites and verify the actual result. No generic recovery platform, dedicated outside-mobile F12 emergency circuit or exhaustive hypothetical-failure runbook is required.

#### Security and recovery of effects

- **FR-015**: Every private read or protected action MUST check applicable rights for the account, action and resource independently of the interface. An absent permission or unusable evidence MUST NOT grant implicit rights; still-valid evidence and bounded tolerances remain defined by F05/F09. Operational and administrative permissions belong to F12 and each domain. F12 FR-015–FR-018 requires a complete check at every cold start, with explicit retry after failure and no persisted access-configuration fallback. Foreground/authentication returns, partial administrative reads and changes do not establish a new successful startup check; current-process restrictions and revisions remain protected. A refusal MUST leave data unchanged.
- **FR-016**: Sign-out or an account change MUST prevent private data, rights and deferred responses from the previous session from being exposed or applied to the next. Retrying after sign-in MUST use the current authorized context; no local retention replaces F05 identity and session rules.
- **FR-017**: Secrets, protection copies and recovery operations MUST remain accessible only to authorized people or operations, and sensitive exchanges MUST be protected. An unauthorized or abusive request MUST NOT bypass rights or trigger unbounded work. Technical protection limits are derived from the usage profile and verified without imposing a provider or expanding business roles.
- **FR-018**: Qualification MUST include refusals without effects for a visitor, an account without permission, another owner, an invalidated session and a response from an old session. Evidence MUST distinguish effective operation protection from merely hiding the interface. F05 owns the accepted identity-provider session and account-return limits; this qualification adds neither stronger revocation guarantees nor a separate session-management requirement. Revocation, deletion, Pro tolerance and identity rules remain those of F05/F09/F11; their specific validation cannot be replaced by a generic sign-in check.
- **FR-019**: Ordinary repeated submissions, authorized retries and reconstruction MUST preserve business identity, current permissions and accepted data without unintended duplicate effects or stale overwrite. Domain-specific delivery limits apply, including F07 system-delivery limits and F10 exceptional duplicate issues resolved manually. A lost response is not proof of cancellation. Ordinary explicit retries may repeat an attempt; existing expected revisions, unique constraints, desired-state commands and domain confirmation protect actual effects. Specialized irreversible provider operations retain their supported evidence/manual handling. No universal operation receipt, retry ledger or automatic recovery protocol is required for every command. F10 explicitly permits a new attempt without mobile receipt lookup, accepting rare duplicate issues for manual closure. F05 specifically accepts ordinary provider errors/manual handling for simultaneous cleanup and return; original-business-UUID targeting and private-data isolation remain required.
- **FR-020**: Rebuilding derived data MUST use authoritative data and preserve usable prior results until complete replacement is established. A partial or failed rebuild MUST NOT publish an empty or incomplete result as complete. Without usable prior state, unavailability MUST be explicit. In every case, current visibility and entitlement restrictions MUST apply; no indexing or storage mechanism is chosen.
- **FR-021**: Partial work MUST retain accepted successes and expose useful failures or missing results without claiming completion. The next ordinary collection, a standard rerun or authorized manual intervention may complete the relevant work; fine-grained crash resumption and a generic recovery engine are not required. F01/F02 eligibility, authority and current restrictions still apply. Rebuilding derivatives MUST NOT alter authoritative data to fit an expected result.

#### Backups and restoration

- **FR-022**: Authoritative user/operator data, including follows, settings, corrections and logo associations, and retained required image bytes MUST be backed up every 30 minutes, with 30-day retention. This is a scheduling policy, not a zero-loss or maximum-loss guarantee during failures; the last usable copy determines recovery. Acquired sporting data has no separate four-hour loss requirement: ordinary collection can reconstruct only what sources still publish. Irreplaceable files, user changes and unavailable source history MUST NOT be presumed reconstructible. Fully derived projections may be rebuilt from retained authoritative data. F10 support cases and native attachments are the explicit exception: GitHub is their sole storage, with no independent Blockout backup; recovery depends on the provider.
- **FR-023**: Copies needed for restoration MUST be retained for 30 days, independently of the production host and its loss, with restricted access and verifiable content. A failed new copy MUST NOT delete the last usable copy. This protection retention MUST NOT become a retention period for active data or diagnostics; F11 constraints remain applicable, including to erased data and expired copies.
- **FR-024**: Preserve retained irreplaceable file bytes and their associations, including required club logos and authorized attachments, consistently with restorable data. F10 GitHub-native support attachments follow the provider-owned exception in FR-022. Database backups, stored URLs or external hosting alone do not demonstrate file recovery. Use appropriate standard storage protection; no custom file-backup engine, per-write remote durability fence or storage versioning technology is prescribed by this requirement. F11 privacy rules remain applicable.
- **FR-025**: Backup operation MUST use ordinary scheduled backups without required notifications, monitoring dashboards, automatic verification or a last-success supervisor. The operator MAY inspect existing backup-tool history and destination copies manually when in doubt. Failed jobs or a stopped schedule may remain unnoticed; no detection guarantee is claimed.
