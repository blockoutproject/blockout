# Functional specification: shared quality and operations

**Feature branch**: `feature/259-shared-quality`

**Created**: 2026-09-17

**Status**: Accepted functional baseline under [planning authority #9](https://github.com/blockoutproject/blockout/issues/9); technical-dossier acceptance and implementation evidence remain separate.

**Requested scope**: Approved functional quality and operations for [#259](https://github.com/blockoutproject/blockout-legacy/issues/259), F13 in the [specification navigation](../../README.md#specifications), as amended by the owner. Preserve F01/F02 behavior, protect irreplaceable data/files and support ordinary manual operations. F14 owns V1 club/logo continuity. No performance/capacity qualification or monthly availability accounting is retained.

## Clarifications — approved KISS revision, 2026-10-06

- Q: What is the retained operations baseline? → A: Dokploy PostgreSQL backups every 30 minutes and hourly periodic AWS Backup for images, each with 30-day retention. Use native problem notifications/history without last-success absence thresholds or continuous-protection configuration monitoring. Silent scheduling stops may go unalerted. Keep independent restore proof and privacy obligations. Every new application delivery qualifies its own source-matching candidate; promote the same images without rebuilding. Documents alone trigger no application delivery or old-candidate reuse optimization.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-05

- Q: What backup and recovery baseline replaces custom recovery machinery? → A: Use standard scheduled Dokploy database backups to AWS, initially every 30 minutes with 30-day retention, and manual restoration. Collection has no separate four-hour loss objective; source availability limits later reconstruction.
- Q: Do performance goals remain deferred work? → A: No. Remove concurrency/latency targets, load tests, benchmarks and capacity planning from the present scope, release gates and roadmap; create no deferred work item. Focus on a correct application and production delivery, retaining ordinary diagnostics and domain-owned timing rules.
- Q: Is monthly availability accounting required? → A: No. Remove the monthly availability target, calculation and proof obligations; retain actionable incident detection, alerts and manual operations.
- Q: Must every failure have an automated recovery protocol? → A: No. Keep ordinary correctness and useful domain state for important incomplete actions; standard tools, monitoring and authorized manual intervention are the baseline. F10 permits exceptional manually resolved duplicate issues.

### Session 2026-10-04

- Q: Does zero duplicate incident notification require exactly-once transport delivery? → A: One logical incident opening, no deliberate repeat reminders, and a notification on confirmed recovery are required. Exceptional transport duplicates after a lost network response are accepted; the delivery limits must be documented and tested.

## User scenarios and validation _(mandatory)_

Visitors and signed-in users view sporting data; authorized users perform actions within their domain. The operator receives alerts and conducts recovery according to their permissions and availability. Tools and providers are implementation means, not additional business roles.

The scenarios describe evidence to obtain. They declare neither an implemented V2 nor an already demonstrated load test, production availability or restoration. Identifiers are local to this specification and do not constitute technical tasks.

### User story 1 — Consult correct, current authorised data (Priority: P1)

Users consult sporting details, calendars and search with accepted changes reflected
in the relevant views, without restrictions being bypassed or supplier failure being
presented as a sporting deletion.

**Priority rationale**: Correct content and understandable failures are required for
ordinary use; no workload, latency benchmark or capacity qualification is required.

**Independent validation**: Accept a correction, consult/refresh its related views,
then exercise a supplier failure and a visibility restriction.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A01** — Withdrawn: synthetic workload and latency qualification are outside scope, not deferred release work. (FR-001–FR-003)
2. **A02** — **Given** an accepted sporting correction, **when** affected views are consulted/refreshed and derived projection processing completes, **then** they use the accepted state rather than restoring an older version. Current visibility and permissions apply even before a projection catches up; no continuous screen refresh or global propagation stopwatch is required. (FR-004, FR-005)
3. **A03** — **Given** an unavailable supplier and retained consultable data, **when** a user opens its details or calendar, **then** the accepted data remains usable under F02 with honest freshness. The supplier failure is visible to operations, not interpreted as sporting absence or successful integration. (FR-005, FR-008)

### User story 2 — Understand incidents and intervene without mandatory on-call duty (Priority: P1)

As an independent operator, I want actionable alerts and the information needed to intervene when I am available.

**Priority rationale**: Actionable incidents support manual intervention; unavailable supervision does not establish healthy service.

**Independent validation**: Exercise bounded incidents and inspect alerts, diagnostics and practical recovery instructions; no monthly availability calculation.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A04** — Withdrawn: monthly availability calculation and its proof are outside scope. Incident detection remains covered by A05–A07. (FR-006, FR-007)
2. **A05** — **Given** significant unavailability, including loss of the VPS or its local monitoring, **when** it is detected, **then** an alert can reach the operator without depending on that VPS functioning. Detection time, scope and last known state are available. The operator intervenes according to availability; no guaranteed intervention time is announced. (FR-009–FR-011)
3. **A06** — **Given** an open F01 acquisition incident, **when** identical failures persist or the family is paused, **then** no new opening notification or false recovery notification is produced. A new observation establishing recovery of the same scope closes it once. An integration failure that remains open is not resolved by acquisition success alone. (FR-010, FR-012)
4. **A07** — **Given** partial integration or a refused retry, **when** the operator views diagnostics and instructions, **then** they distinguish successful elements, failed elements, affected scope, reason and authorized recovery action. Useful information exposes no secret, personal data or raw provider document. (FR-010, FR-013, FR-014)

### User story 3 — Preserve rights and action effects (Priority: P1)

As a user, I want failures and retries neither to give another account access to my data nor to allow unauthorized actions, and not to duplicate an already accepted action.

**Priority rationale**: Cached information never replaces current authorisation decisions.

**Independent validation**: Submit authorized and prohibited requests, change accounts during a deferred response, then replay an operation whose confirmation was lost.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A08** — **Given** a visitor, an account without the required right or a request concerning another account’s data, **when** a protected action is requested directly or from an old interface, **then** it is refused without a prohibited effect. Absent or unusable entitlement evidence grants no implicit right; still-valid evidence and tolerances explicitly defined by F05/F09 remain applicable. (FR-015, FR-017, FR-018)
2. **A09** — **Given** a still-pending response intended for account A, **when** the user signs out or uses account B, **then** this response does not populate session B and account A’s private data or rights do not remain usable by B. (FR-016, FR-018)
3. **A10** — **Given** an ordinary accepted action whose response was lost, **when** its result is reread or a safe authorized retry occurs, **then** ordinary duplicate effects are prevented and uncertainty is not called cancellation. Apply domain-specific exceptional delivery limits, including manual resolution of a rare duplicate F10 issue. No generic recovery protocol is an acceptance condition. (FR-008, FR-019)

### User story 4 — Recover complete state after interrupted processing (Priority: P1)

As an operator, I want to rerun processing or rebuild derived data without losing correct results or bringing obsolete state back into use.

**Priority rationale**: Partially rebuilt search must not be presented as a complete empty result.

**Independent validation**: Interrupt integration and rebuilding at several stages, replay observations and compare retained data, identities and final visibility.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A11** — **Given** accepted partial results and a visible failure, **when** the next normal collection, standard rerun or authorized manual correction occurs, **then** accepted data and identities are preserved, current restrictions apply and completion is reported honestly. Resuming the exact interrupted step is not required. (FR-019, FR-021)
2. **A12** — **Given** usable search and an interrupted rebuild, **when** derived data has been partially prepared, **then** it does not replace usable results with an empty or incomplete state presented as complete. Currently hidden or prohibited data is not re-exposed through retained results. Without usable prior state, unavailability is explicit rather than a false “no results”. (FR-020)
3. **A13** — **Given** an old observation or work interrupted before a division restriction, **when** it is replayed, **then** it does not overwrite newer accepted state or bypass current restrictions. Rebuilding alone does not re-emit processed announcements; F07 accepts an exceptional duplicate if restoration lost match-level facts. (FR-019–FR-021)

### User story 5 — Restore retained data and files (Priority: P1)

As an operator, I want actually restorable backups, with an explicit recovered scope and files consistent with the restored data.

**Priority rationale**: The presence of a database backup or file URL does not demonstrate recovery of the whole set.

**Independent validation**: Restore copies in an isolated environment, making original storage unavailable, and check actual recovered scope, files, rights and applicable obligations using standard tools and manual review.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A14** — **Given** scheduled independent backups with an initial 30-minute interval, **when** production storage is lost and a usable copy is restored manually, **then** user/operator data and required files are checked against that copy and any loss is explicit. Acquired data has no separate four-hour loss bound; new collection reconstructs only what sources still publish. An old logo URL alone does not preserve its bytes. (FR-022–FR-024)
2. **A15** — **Given** scheduled PostgreSQL and image protection, **when** a job reports failure, **then** native problem notifications and tool history identify it. If scheduling silently stops, no absence-of-success alarm is promised. Verify completed copies and actual recovery through the existing tools; normal retention still expires old copies. (FR-023, FR-025)
3. **A16** — **Given** copies independent of the VPS and original storage made unavailable in the exercise, **when** restoration is performed before launch, **then** expected data and its files are recovered, derivatives can be rebuilt and gaps are identified. Operation duration and observed limits are measured; the exercise does not prove round-the-clock human intervention availability. (FR-024, FR-026)
4. **A17** — **Given** a backup predating applicable erasure, rights changes or notification purge, **when** restoring it manually, **then** the operator checks and applies current obligations before affected access or sends resume. Missing evidence leaves affected capabilities unavailable. No automatic reconstruction of all interrupted operations is required. (FR-027; F05/F09/F11 and F07 A51)
5. **A18** — **Given** a failing application version, **when** returning to a previous version is considered, **then** its compatibility with retained data is checked. An image rollback claims neither to undo a data change nor to replace restoration; incompatibility prevents this unsafe rollback and directs the operator to the documented recovery procedure. After V2 opens, F14 requires its recovery without requiring a functional return to V1. (FR-014, FR-028)

### User story 6 — Use the application with the phone’s capabilities (Priority: P2)

As an iOS or Android user, I want French content, accessible actions and understandable waiting or error states.

**Priority rationale**: A quick response is not useful if it is unreadable, inaccessible or misleading on failure.

**Independent validation**: Browse representative screens on both platforms with screen reading, text enlargement, reduced motion and connection loss.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A19** — **Given** a supported iOS or Android phone, **when** the user browses the application, **then** Blockout-owned content and errors are in French, without changing source names or sporting identifiers. Dates and times retain F02/F03 meaning. (FR-029)
2. **A20** — **Given** screen reading, enlarged text or reduced motion enabled, **when** the user follows representative journeys, **then** actions have an understandable name, role and state; essential information remains readable and errors identifiable without depending on color or animation alone. (FR-030)
3. **A21** — **Given** lost connectivity or failed loading, **when** the user views data or submits an action, **then** they can leave the waiting state and distinguish retained data, absent data, error and an uncertain outcome. A retry respects current rights and does not repeat indefinitely. No new offline editing capability is implicit. (FR-008, FR-016, FR-019, FR-031)

### User story 7 — Preserve logo associations during transition (Priority: P1)

As an operator who has associated club logos, I want the authorized reset not to destroy this manual work.

**Priority rationale**: Files are retained in S3, but their club association resides in the V1 database; losing that association may make the files unusable.

**Independent validation**: Examine a controlled recovery set with an identified club, an ambiguous association and a missing file, according to F02 rules and the transition prerequisites handed to F14.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A22** — **Given** associations between V1 club references and logo URLs, **when** their protection and recovery are verified for F14, **then** each expected association and file is retained or explicitly flagged as missing. A certain correspondence may be reapplied; an absent or ambiguous correspondence remains retained for resolution without an assumed link. S3 availability alone does not prove retention of associations. (FR-024, FR-032; F02 FR-002, FR-043, FR-046)

### Edge cases

- Traffic anecdotes are historical context, not workload requirements or capacity evidence.
- A fast error, hidden data served from a cache or obsolete information presented as fresh does not satisfy a quality objective.
- Timely collection, successful integration and propagation to views remain three distinct observations.
- A monitoring gap is not evidence of availability; an external incident and a Blockout outage are not confused when they have different effects on journeys.
- A backup stored outside the VPS may still be incomplete or unusable; file deletion may invalidate an older database copy.
- Restoration may recover rights or data that have become prohibited since the copy. Current restrictions must precede making them available again.
- An old club absent from rebuilt competitions retains its logo association in recovery material; that association alone does not create a new viewable club.

## Requirements _(mandatory)_

### Functional requirements

#### Functional quality and freshness

- **FR-001**: Withdrawn: no concurrency or action-rate target is retained in the current scope or deferred roadmap.
- **FR-002**: Withdrawn: no load-test, benchmark or capacity-study protocol is required for this scope or release.
- **FR-003**: Withdrawn: no numerical API latency or percentile acceptance target is retained. Ordinary correctness and bounded user waits remain required by FR-008.
- **FR-004**: Accepted sporting changes MUST update their affected views and derived projections. New consultation/refresh MUST respect current permissions and visibility; a stale projection never authorises disclosure. Keep freshness honest and update failed projections through normal processing/manual correction. No global numerical propagation objective or continuous screen refresh is required.
- **FR-005**: Distinguish supplier observation, acquisition, accepted integration and completed projection. Success at one step MUST NOT prove the next; failed or pending integration/projection remains diagnosable. F01 collection timing and domain-owned expiry/freshness rules remain applicable without a global performance qualification.

#### Incident detection and operations

- **FR-006**: Withdrawn: no monthly availability target or accounting is required. Incident detection and safe operations remain under FR-008–FR-014.
- **FR-007**: Withdrawn: no monthly availability report, coverage calculation or retained proof series is required. Missing diagnostics MUST NOT be represented as healthy operation under FR-010.
- **FR-008**: A failure MUST remain isolated to capabilities that actually depend on it. Previously retained data that remains viewable MUST remain usable under F02, with its freshness state. The user MUST distinguish empty results, loading, errors and uncertain action outcomes, be able to leave the waiting state and have a retry option when safe. Waits and automatic attempts MUST be bounded; their technical settings MUST NOT allow waiting that blocks navigation indefinitely or invented success.
- **FR-009**: Important incidents MUST automatically alert the operator: essential-journey failure, failed integration/reconstruction or projection, failed protection and F01 acquisition incidents. Host-loss detection and alert transmission MUST NOT depend only on that VPS. Document and verify the selected monitoring configuration; no monthly availability accounting is required.
- **FR-010**: The operator MUST be able to know the affected scope, time and outcome of the last observation, last success, pending or failed work and available partial-success counts. Never run, awaiting eligibility, paused, in progress, completed and failed states MUST remain distinguishable. Inaccessible monitoring or execution without a confirmed outcome MUST NOT be presented as healthy.
- **FR-011**: Recovery from a significant incident MUST allow authorized manual intervention according to operator availability. No guaranteed human deadline or permanent on-call duty is required. Detection, alerting, start of intervention and recovery MUST remain distinct in operational information to measure durations without hiding the wait for human intervention.
- **FR-012**: Reports of the same problem and scope MUST be grouped, with an opening notification and a notification of actual recovery, without a new opening on every attempt or periodic reminders. Uniqueness applies to logical incident transitions; exceptional notification transport duplicates after a lost response are accepted, with documented and tested delivery limits. F01-specific thresholds and recovery evidence MUST be preserved. Success in another scope, a pause or successful acquisition with integration still failing MUST NOT close the affected incident. Distinct problems remain identifiable.
- **FR-013**: Diagnostics MUST allow failure stages and scope to be linked using non-personal technical references, times, outcome categories and actionable reasons. They MUST NOT expose secrets, tokens, personal data, attachments or raw provider documents. User-facing errors MUST be understandable and offer an appropriate next step without internal details. F11 FR-021, FR-026–FR-027 defines purposes and retention constraints without setting a numeric logging duration; no logging duration is inferred from the 30 backup days.
- **FR-014**: The operator MUST have concise usable instructions for relevant standard operations: interpreting alerts, checking backups, manually resolving important incomplete actions, rebuilding derived data, restoring and evaluating a compatible rollback. State access prerequisites and verify the actual result. No generic recovery platform, dedicated outside-mobile F12 emergency circuit or exhaustive hypothetical-failure runbook is required.

#### Security and recovery of effects

- **FR-015**: Every private read or protected action MUST check applicable rights for the account, action and resource independently of the interface. An absent permission or unusable evidence MUST NOT grant implicit rights; still-valid evidence and bounded tolerances remain defined by F05/F09. Operational and administrative permissions belong to F12 and each domain. F12 FR-015–FR-018 requires a complete check at every cold start, with explicit retry after failure and no persisted access-configuration fallback. Foreground/authentication returns, partial administrative reads and changes do not establish a new successful startup check; current-process restrictions and revisions remain protected. A refusal MUST leave data unchanged.
- **FR-016**: Sign-out or an account change MUST prevent private data, rights and deferred responses from the previous session from being exposed or applied to the next. Retrying after sign-in MUST use the current authorized context; no local retention replaces F05 identity and session rules.
- **FR-017**: Secrets, protection copies and recovery operations MUST remain accessible only to authorized people or operations, and sensitive exchanges MUST be protected. An unauthorized or abusive request MUST NOT bypass rights or trigger unbounded work. Technical protection limits are derived from the usage profile and verified without imposing a provider or expanding business roles.
- **FR-018**: Qualification MUST include refusals without effects for a visitor, an account without permission, another owner, an invalidated session and a response from an old session. Evidence MUST distinguish effective operation protection from merely hiding the interface. F05 defines standard Auth0 token validity and its accepted same-subject return limitation; this qualification does not require a business session registry or retroactive JWT invalidation. Revocation, deletion, Pro tolerance and identity rules remain those of F05/F09/F11; their specific validation cannot be replaced by a generic sign-in check.
- **FR-019**: Ordinary repeated submissions, authorized retries and reconstruction MUST preserve business identity, current permissions and accepted data without unintended duplicate effects or stale overwrite. Domain-specific delivery limits apply, including F07 system-delivery limits and F10 exceptional duplicate issues resolved manually. A lost response is not proof of cancellation; use current state, supported provider evidence or authorized manual intervention before repeating an unsafe effect. No universal operation receipt, retry ledger or automatic recovery protocol is required for every command.
- **FR-020**: Rebuilding derived data MUST use authoritative data and preserve usable prior results until complete replacement is established. A partial or failed rebuild MUST NOT publish an empty or incomplete result as complete. Without usable prior state, unavailability MUST be explicit. In every case, current visibility and entitlement restrictions MUST apply; no indexing or storage mechanism is chosen.
- **FR-021**: Partial work MUST retain accepted successes and expose useful failures or missing results without claiming completion. The next ordinary collection, a standard rerun or authorized manual intervention may complete the relevant work; fine-grained crash resumption and a generic recovery engine are not required. F01/F02 eligibility, authority and current restrictions still apply. Rebuilding derivatives MUST NOT alter authoritative data to fit an expected result.

#### Backups and restoration

- **FR-022**: User/operator data, including follows, settings, corrections and logo associations, MUST be included in PostgreSQL backups every 30 minutes. Image bytes MUST have hourly periodic AWS Backup protection. Both retain copies for 30 days. This is a scheduling policy, not a zero-loss or guaranteed maximum-loss promise during failed backups; the last usable copy determines actual recovery. Acquired sporting data has no separate four-hour loss requirement: ordinary collection may reconstruct data still published by its sources. Irreplaceable files, user changes and unavailable source history MUST NOT be presumed reconstructible. Fully derived projections may be rebuilt from retained authoritative data.
- **FR-023**: Copies needed for restoration MUST be retained for 30 days, independently of the production VPS and its loss, with restricted access and verifiable content. A failed new copy MUST NOT delete the last usable copy. This protection retention MUST NOT become a retention period for active data or diagnostics; F11 constraints remain applicable, including to erased data and expired copies.
- **FR-024**: Preserve retained irreplaceable file bytes and their associations, including required club logos and authorized attachments, consistently with restorable data. Database backups, stored URLs or external hosting alone do not demonstrate file recovery. Use appropriate standard storage protection; no custom file-backup engine, per-write remote durability fence or storage versioning technology is prescribed by this requirement. F11 privacy rules remain applicable.
- **FR-025**: Operators MUST use native backup problem notifications and tool history to inspect completed jobs, scope and failures. A started job is not a completed backup; qualify actual Dokploy completion including S3 upload and AWS periodic-job notifications. No elapsed-success threshold, Blockout counter or additional supervisor is required. A silently stopped schedule may produce no alert: this limitation is accepted and must be documented. Failed jobs do not remove the last usable copy before its normal expiry. Recovery is automatic only with positive evidence; otherwise use operator resolution in existing tools.
- **FR-026**: Before relying on backups operationally, demonstrate a manual restoration in an isolated environment using the selected standard tools. Check retained data, required files, applicable rights/privacy and rebuilt derivatives; record actual gaps and duration. Repeat relevant checks after a material restoration change. No restoration-time or human-response guarantee, custom recovery service or exhaustive failure-injection platform is required.
- **FR-027**: Before restored data becomes usable, apply current erasure, identity, rights and privacy obligations under F05/F09/F11 and retained F07 expiry/announcement rules. Standard provider checks and authorized manual review/correction may establish the result. Keep affected access or sends unavailable when those obligations cannot be established. This does not require automatic reconstruction of every interrupted step, a synchronous independent receiver or a general zero-loss effect journal.
- **FR-028**: Returning to a previous application version MUST retain data and verify compatibility with its current state before resuming service. Application replacement MUST NOT be presented as automatically reversing data changes. When compatible rollback is impossible, instructions MUST explain the recovery path and necessary checks; initial V1/V2 cutover and its specific criteria remain in F14. After V2 opens, F14 requires its repair or restoration without requiring a functional return to V1; returning to a compatible application version within the V2 cycle remains governed by this requirement.

#### Platforms, accessibility and bounded continuity

- **FR-029**: The shared foundation MUST cover supported iOS and Android phones and Blockout content in French. Official names and source content remain faithful to their source; date, time and day semantics belong to F02/F03. No web application, dedicated tablet experience or additional translation is required. The actual matrix of qualified versions and devices MUST be declared; F12 governs minimum versions and update journeys.
- **FR-030**: Essential actions and information MUST be usable with platform screen readers, system text enlargement and reduced motion. Controls MUST present an understandable name, role and state, a touch target of at least 44 logical points, and consistent reading and focus order; essential text MUST NOT be truncated in a way that prevents action. An error, selection or essential information MUST NOT depend solely on color, an unexplained gesture or animation. Contrast and states are checked in visual review R02 without declaring an unperformed certification.
- **FR-031**: Journeys MUST retain consistent states for loading, absence, retained data, error, refusal, action in progress and retry, with usable navigation and return. Retained information remains subject to current rights. Network disconnection MUST NOT be presented as an empty list or successful modification. No complete offline mode, new offline action queue or continuous result stream is required; F03–F10 define their specific journeys.
- **FR-032**: Protection and restoration checks MUST account for club-logo associations and files whose continuity F02 requires. F14 MUST have evidence of their independent retention before any reset, explicitly distinguishing reapplied associations, associations awaiting a match and missing files. F02 identity rules prohibit automatic matching solely by name. FR-022 scheduled protection MUST NOT authorize deliberate loss of associations during this prepared transition. A reported missing file MUST NOT count as evidence of retention or authorization to reset. The approved manual recovery scope is limited to club logos; this specification executes neither exports, associations nor resets.

### Main entities

- **Quality objective**: Expected outcome, scope, period and measurement conditions; distinct from an actual measurement or intervention guarantee.
- **Operational observation**: Scope, date, state, last success, partial results and actionable reasons, without personal data.
- **Incident**: A bounded problem with opening, observations and recovery evidence; user incidents and business notifications remain distinct.
- **Restorable set**: Consistent data and files recoverable from independent copies, with date, protected categories and applicable access or erasure constraints.
- **Logo association to preserve**: Club identity reference and link to the retained file; F02 defines identity and F14 recovery between V1 and V2.

## Success criteria _(mandatory)_

### Measurable outcomes

- **SC-001**: Withdrawn with A01 and FR-001–FR-003. No performance or capacity evidence is a release condition or deferred task.
- **SC-002**: A02/A03 verify accepted changes across affected views, current restrictions and honest freshness. Supplier failure never becomes sporting deletion or integration success. No global propagation timing benchmark is required.
- **SC-003**: Withdrawn with A04 and FR-006–FR-007. A05–A07 retain actual incident detection and diagnostics, without monthly availability accounting.
- **SC-004**: A05–A07 distinguish each failing scope, partial success and actual recovery. Incident detection and notification do not depend solely on the monitored VPS and produce zero duplicate logical opening transitions or recovery transitions without evidence. Exceptional transport duplicates following a lost response are accepted and reported separately; no exactly-once notification transport guarantee is claimed. Manual intervention and its duration remain measured without a guaranteed response time.
- **SC-005**: A08–A13 prevent unauthorized access, cross-account leaks, ordinary duplicate business effects, stale overwrite and incomplete reconstructions presented as complete. Apply each domain's explicit exceptional-delivery limits. Uncertainty never becomes invented success, and useful manual resolution does not require a generic recovery engine.
- **SC-006**: A14–A18 verify PostgreSQL backups every 30 minutes, hourly periodic image backups, independent 30-day retention, native problem notifications and recoverable required files. An unnoticed stop of scheduled jobs may produce no alert; no last-success threshold or Blockout supervisor is required. Test a practical isolated restore and report actual loss/duration without a zero-loss or intervention-time guarantee. Check current rights, erasure and privacy obligations before affected access resumes; these future qualifications are NOT EXECUTED.
- **SC-007**: A19–A21 cover both platforms and declared accessibility modes, with zero essential actions inaccessible due to a missing label, truncated text or information conveyed solely by color/animation; offline states remain distinct from business outcomes.
- **SC-008**: In A22, each logo association in the recovery scope has an explicit outcome and a retained file or reported absence; zero ambiguous associations are applied and zero unresolved associations are silently discarded. F14 requires verified retention before any reset.

## Assumptions

- Historical V1 observation: traffic and independent VPS operation were reported by the owner. They neither prescribe capacity nor prove runtime behavior. Native scheduled backups and manual restoration remain explicit constraints; no performance or monthly availability work is deferred.
- V1 club logos are hosted in S3 and referenced by URL in the database. This demonstrates neither protection against deletion nor complete restorability; that evidence remains required.
- Limiting, monitoring, alert delivery, backup, rebuild consistency and orchestration mechanisms are subsequent technical choices. V1 tool names do not impose a V2 topology.
- Keep failures and unresolved important work visible. No absolute provider availability, human response deadline, synthetic performance target or on-call duty is added.
- Functional evidence and representative native-device checks are defined by the owning technical plans without changing approved journeys or adding performance gates. F11 owns privacy; F05/F09 own identity and rights checks for operations and restoration.
- Global functional and visual review under [#247](https://github.com/blockoutproject/blockout-legacy/issues/247) precedes any technical planning. This specification authorizes no production change, migration, provider load test, provider choice or new administration console.
