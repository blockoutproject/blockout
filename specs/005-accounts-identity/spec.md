# Functional specification: accounts and identity

**Feature branch**: `feature/261-accounts-identity`

**Created on**: 2026-09-18

**Status**: Accepted functional baseline under [planning authority #9](https://github.com/blockoutproject/blockout/issues/9); technical-dossier acceptance and implementation evidence remain separate.

**Requested scope**: [#261](https://github.com/blockoutproject/blockout-legacy/issues/261), F05 in the [specification navigation](../../README.md#specifications). Define guest access, onboarding, sign-in, profile creation and editing, already linked identities, sessions, account-age evidence and voluntary deletion through a possible return. Detailed paid entitlements belong to F09; migration belongs to F14.

## Clarifications — approved KISS revision, 2026-10-06

- Q: Does sharing an email create an identity conflict? → A: No. Distinct Auth0 principals may share it; identity associations, single creation per principal and no email-based merge remain mandatory. RevenueCat SDK state determines Pro; the backend owns account mappings and cleanup only. Native restoration accepts its standard transfer scope.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-05

- Q: Does account erasure require a generic automated recovery framework? → A: No. Record the accepted request, a single deletion state and original references needed for cleanup; attempt ordinary cleanup automatically and handle exceptional incomplete cleanup manually with existing tools, without a persisted per-step ledger. Current-account isolation, honest results and protection of a later account remain required.

## User scenarios and validation _(mandatory)_

The actors are the visitor, the authenticated person, the account holder and authorized personnel handling a support request or blocked deletion. Possession of an email address or a claimed identifier does not prove account ownership. A moderation permission grants neither access to another person’s profile nor manual Pro benefits. F12 assigns permissions; each personal action remains limited to its owner.

The scenarios describe a future implementation, not tests already executed. A, FR and SC references are local to F05. Qualification accounts and evidence remain private, without personal identifiers in public deliverables.

### User story 1 — Access the product and recover my account (Priority: P1)

As a visitor or account holder, I want to browse public content and recover my account using my usual method, without a mandatory profile form or unexpected linking.

**Priority rationale**: Identity determines personal actions and entitlement continuity; a sign-in failure must not close public content or support.

**Independent validation**: Exercise first access, both selected providers, an already linked identity, two principals sharing an address and missing provider information, with and without an existing business profile.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A01** — **Given** an installation with no signed-in account, **when** the person chooses guest access or skips onboarding, **then** public content, legal documents and support remain accessible; no personal action or Pro benefit from the previous account is granted. An account-only action offers sign-in without being implicitly executed. Ordinary F12 restrictions still apply; legal access, support and authentication needed by the operator remain reachable without implicitly opening the application. (FR-001, FR-002)
2. **A02** — **Given** onboarding is presented, **when** the person completes it, skips it or refuses notifications, **then** they can continue. Its completion is retained on the installation and is not reset by sign-out or solely by recreating the business profile during migration. A new installation may present it. The separate, dismissible F14 transition welcome does not replay this onboarding. (FR-002)
3. **A03** — **Given** a first Google or Apple sign-in and a usable provider address, **when** the identity is authenticated, **then** a single usable profile is created automatically, with a neutral valid username and the default avatar, without retrieving a provider photo; no mandatory additional form is requested. Two concurrent retries for the same identity find the same account. (FR-003, FR-004, FR-012, FR-013)
4. **A04** — **Given** an Auth0 principal with Google and Apple identities already linked, even with different addresses, **when** the person signs in with each, before or after business profile recreation, **then** they find the same account and its applicable entitlements. A secondary identity or historical purchase identifier does not constitute a new business account. On transition to V2, explicit F14 reauthentication resets neither this continuity nor account age. (FR-005, FR-010, FR-023)
5. **A05** — **Given** two distinct, unlinked Auth0 principals supplying the same usable email, **when** each explicitly establishes its profile, **then** two distinct business accounts are allowed, including case differences in the email. Neither profile is merged or disclosed to the other. Concurrent creation for one principal still produces only one account. (FR-004, FR-006, FR-007)
6. **A06** — **Given** two unlinked identities using distinct addresses, including an Apple private address, **when** they are used, **then** no personal equivalence is inferred; each eligible account remains distinct. Two populated accounts are neither merged nor emptied by a support request or purchase restoration. (FR-004, FR-006, FR-007, FR-036)
7. **A07** — **Given** first creation without a usable address or with failed provider retrieval, **when** sign-in reaches profile creation, **then** creation remains suspended, without a fabricated address or an additional verification form. Retry, support and guest access are offered. No incomplete business profile is announced as ready. (FR-008, FR-017, FR-018)
8. **A08** — **Given** a recognized identity with an existing profile, **when** provider email is temporarily missing, **then** the reliable stored value remains. A usable updated email may also belong to another principal: it is synchronized without merging accounts. Loading failure remains an unavailable state. (FR-007–FR-009, FR-018)

### User story 2 — Use and customize a minimal profile (Priority: P1)

As an account holder, I want a useful profile without unnecessarily copying my personal information, and to change my username or photo without loss on failure.

**Priority rationale**: Creation must remain simple, customization reliable and data proportionate to the selected functions.

**Independent validation**: Create a profile, edit its fields separately, trigger a username conflict and a photo failure, then sign in again with the identity.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A09** — **Given** a provider also returning last name, first name and phone number, **when** the business profile is created or synchronized, **then** only username, email and photo constitute its personal presentation and contact information. References required for identity, entitlements and lifecycle remain separate; no deletion of the Google/Apple profile is inferred from this minimization. (FR-011, FR-012)
2. **A10** — **Given** username candidates that are too short, too long, contain forbidden characters or are already used, **when** a username is generated, **then** the result follows the same rules as manual input. When two people simultaneously request the same username regardless of letter case, at most one obtains it; the other keeps their old username and can correct it, or receives another valid username during automatic creation. (FR-012, FR-013)
3. **A11** — **Given** a customized profile, **when** the holder changes only their username and then signs in again, **then** the photo and customized username are preserved except for the requested change. An invalid or conflicting username is rejected without changing the profile. An attempt to edit, privately read or delete another person’s account is refused. (FR-004, FR-013, FR-014, FR-016)
4. **A12** — **Given** an old photo, **when** replacement fails or the file is rejected, **then** the previous photo remains available. Successful explicit deletion removes the photo; a missing photo does not prevent account use. If the outcome of saving is uncertain, a reread establishes the state before announcing success or repeating the operation. (FR-014, FR-015)

### User story 3 — Control my session without mixing accounts (Priority: P1)

As a user, I want to recover after an outage, sign out and switch accounts without exposing my data or assigning my entitlements to someone else.

**Priority rationale**: The sign-in outcome, business data loading and entitlement establishment are distinct; confusing them exposes information or unnecessarily blocks access.

**Independent validation**: Vary authentication, profile and entitlement loading, then switch accounts with delayed responses and a failed provider session closure.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A13** — **Given** sign-in cancelled or failed before authentication, **when** the person returns to the application, **then** no account is announced as signed in and sign-in can be retried; guest access and support remain accessible. No raw technical error is exposed. (FR-003, FR-017, FR-038, FR-039)
2. **A14** — **Given** an authenticated identity but an unavailable Blockout profile, **when** loading fails, **then** the state indicates what is missing and allows retry without requiring new authentication that is still valid. Public content remains accessible, personal actions are suspended and data from a previous account is not used. (FR-017, FR-018, FR-021)
3. **A15** — **Given** an established session, **when** a network outage occurs, **then** it is not treated as revocation and no personal write is announced as accepted without confirmation. **When** nonrenewable expiry or revocation is confirmed, **then** protected actions require reauthentication without preventing public consultation. (FR-019, FR-039)
4. **A16** — **Given** a signed-in account, **when** the person signs out and browser session closure fails, **then** the local session and its personal data are removed despite that failure. The application allows free guest access; it does not claim to sign out the global Google/Apple account or other devices. (FR-020, FR-021, FR-023)
5. **A17** — **Given** account A with profile, follow, notification or entitlement requests in progress, **when** the person switches to account B or guest mode and A’s responses then arrive, **then** none populates the new session. A personal operation not yet accepted does not execute under B. (FR-021, FR-023)
6. **A18** — **Given** a device associated with account A’s notifications, **when** A signs out or B uses the device, **then** A’s destination association is removed or invalidated; any new association concerns B. A failed disassociation is retried and does not count as success; no guarantee of recalling an already delivered message is announced. (FR-022)
7. **A19** — **Given** a signed-in account without usable entitlement evidence, **when** verification fails, **then** all public sporting consultation, including pool maps and full club calendars, remains accessible without advertising or an invitation to repurchase. Retry is possible and waiting never establishes a free or active Pro status. Account switching preserves isolation under FR-023. (FR-023)

### User story 4 — Preserve account age faithful to my account (Priority: P1)

As an existing account holder, I want business profile recreation not to lose my account age for actions that depend on it.

**Priority rationale**: Replacing business storage must not be confused with the arrival of a new user.

**Independent validation**: Compare account-age evidence for a single identity, already linked identities and an account recreated after voluntary deletion, with the provider available and then unavailable.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A20** — **Given** an Auth0 principal existing for more than seven days and a business profile created today, **when** account age is requested, **then** the evidence corresponds to the retained principal, regardless of which already linked method is used. F08 applies its threshold and other conditions; no person’s age is inferred. (FR-024)
2. **A21** — **Given** a known date reliably attributed to the principal, **when** the provider is temporarily unavailable, **then** this evidence remains usable. Without a reliable date, no business date or arbitrary value replaces it; publication subject to account age remains suspended with retry, while other authorized functions remain accessible. The moderation exception depends on an established permission, not an invented date. (FR-024, FR-025)
3. **A22** — **Given** a voluntarily deleted account followed by a new registration, **when** account age is assessed, **then** the deleted account’s age is not recovered, even if an address or provider identifier is reused. In contrast, a simple reinstall with the same nondeleted account preserves its age. (FR-024, FR-035)

### User story 5 — Delete my account and return without recovering my old data (Priority: P1)

As an account holder, I want to request deletion from the application, understand its progress and later recover a valid purchase without reactivating my old data.

**Priority rationale**: Deletion crosses multiple recipients; an outage must neither lose a request nor cause resurrection or deletion of a new account.

**Independent validation**: Request deletion with and without a subscription, inject failures before and after acceptance, repeat the request, attempt to return during processing, then create a new account and restore a qualification purchase on each store.

**Acceptance scenarios**:

1. **A23** — **Given** an authenticated holder, possibly subscribed, **when** they open deletion, **then** the irreversible effects, potentially continuing processing and separate store billing are explained, with access to subscription management. Cancelling confirmation preserves the account. Valid confirmation must not be preceded by sign-out that prevents recording the request. (FR-026, FR-027)
2. **A24** — **Given** a request not yet durably accepted, **when** recording it definitively fails, **then** no external deletion has started and the account remains usable; retry is possible. If the response is lost and acceptance uncertain, a check or safe repetition finds the outcome without claiming nothing happened. (FR-027, FR-031)
3. **A25** — **Given** an accepted request, **when** the application closes or Auth0, RevenueCat or file storage becomes unavailable, **then** the targeted account remains unusable on all devices and incomplete cleanup remains findable for authorized manual resolution independently of the application. The interface confirms acceptance, not complete erasure. A Pro account is not exempt from the block. (FR-027–FR-029, FR-031, FR-032)
4. **A26** — **Given** some erasures already completed and others pending, **when** the request or an operation is repeated, **then** absences already achieved cause neither restoration nor an endless loop. Residual access is refused; a persistent outage is visible and addressable by authorized personnel. No complete customer record is retained to facilitate a possible return. (FR-028, FR-029, FR-031, FR-032, FR-038)
5. **A27** — **Given** a deleted account that published a visible link and a hidden link, **when** its data is processed, **then** useful links may remain without association to the account; the hidden link remains hidden. A recreated account does not recover their ownership. Personal data in the content itself falls under an applicable F11 request, without a promise of total anonymization. (FR-030, FR-035, FR-038)
6. **A28** — **Given** deletion still in progress for identity or purchases, **when** the person attempts recreation with the same identity, **then** they find a deletion-in-progress state, guest access and support; no new business creation or restoration exposed to that erasure occurs. Resolution does not require erasure evidence that the provider cannot supply. (FR-032, FR-033)
7. **A29** — **Given** the relevant operations resolved and a new account authorized, **when** an old response, delayed event, erasure retry or backup restoration arrives, **then** the old account does not reappear and the new account and its restored purchases remain protected. Current restrictions are reapplied before restored data is made available again. (FR-031, FR-033–FR-035)
8. **A30** — **Given** completed deletion and a still-valid store subscription, **when** the person creates a new account then restores the purchase with the store account that paid, **then** paid entitlements can be restored without the old profile, follows, contribution attribution or manual benefit. The Google/Apple identity used to sign in to Blockout need not be the one used by the store. Apple and Google journeys are qualified separately. (FR-029, FR-035, FR-036)
9. **A31** — **Given** a purchase associated with another Blockout account, **when** native restoration is explicitly requested, **then** the standard RevenueCat/store transfer scope applies, potentially to the full receipt, without merging Blockout profiles. Qualification establishes actual effects on both accounts. Cross-store impossibility leads to verified F09 assistance, never assignment by email. (FR-036)
10. **A32** — **Given** a person without the application installed or no longer able to sign in, **when** they follow the public deletion link, **then** they find a procedure and contact for requesting erasure without reinstalling the application. Proportionate ownership verification precedes action on the account; the reply address alone does not authorize deletion. Processing provides the status or result under F11. (FR-037, FR-038)

### Edge cases

- Email never identifies or reserves an account. A missing business profile during migration does not invalidate established identity associations. (A04–A08)
- The same identity, username and deletion request may be requested repeatedly or concurrently. The outcome must not depend on a duplicate created by retry. (A03, A10, A24–A26)
- Successful authentication, a ready profile and established Pro entitlements are three distinct outcomes; their responses may arrive after sign-out. (A14–A19)
- A deletion request with a missing response may have been accepted; a confirmed failure before acceptance and an unknown outcome are not equivalent. (A24)
- The provider may accept erasure without having completed it. The account remains blocked during processing, but resolution does not depend on inaccessible evidence. (A25–A29)
- A possibly reused technical identifier grants neither account age, content ownership nor manual benefits from the deleted account. (A22, A27, A29–A30)

## Requirements _(mandatory)_

### Functional requirements

#### Access and identity resolution

- **FR-001**: The system MUST allow guest access to public content, legal documents and support before sign-in or after failure. Personal actions require a usable identity and account; their sign-in prompt MUST NOT automatically execute a purchase, publication, deletion or other mutation. Global F12 access restrictions still apply to ordinary journeys; they preserve legal access, support and authentication needed by the operator without implicitly opening the application.
- **FR-002**: Onboarding MUST be completable or skippable without mandatory account creation or notification permission. Its completion is installation-specific, retained on sign-out and migration business recreation. Its content presents only capabilities retained by the specs, including F06 follows and actual F01 coverage, without promising unsupported club following. The F14 transition welcome is separate: presenting it before sign-in on an installation upgraded from V1 to V2 does not reset that completion.
- **FR-003**: Sign-in MUST use the already selected Google and Apple methods through Auth0, without adding a Blockout password or another provider. Cancellation, failure and success MUST be distinguished and allow appropriate recovery.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST resolve the account from the authenticated identity and its established associations, never from a merely declared email or identifier. Concurrent retries and creations for the same identity MUST lead to a single business account. Actions on an account MUST verify its holder or a permission dedicated to their purpose.
- **FR-005**: Already established Google/Apple associations MUST be preserved, even if their addresses differ. Linked methods find the same business account and its applicable entitlements, independently of a secondary identity or historical payment reference.
- **FR-006**: The system MUST NOT create any new Google/Apple association, even with two authenticated identities or the same address. It MUST neither merge nor delete data from two populated accounts to resolve a conflict. Support follows this rule; recovery of a paid entitlement remains a separate operation.
- **FR-007**: Distinct, unlinked Auth0 principals MAY use the same email, including the same address with different letter case. Each principal resolves its own business account. No email uniqueness reservation, conflict refusal, automatic linking or merge is permitted; email alone never proves ownership.
- **FR-008**: First creation MUST have a usable address from the authenticated identity provider; a missing, unusable or unretrieved address suspends creation. The system offers retry, support and guest access without fabricating an address or adding an input/verification form. Creation occurs only through explicit bootstrap after authentication; an ordinary profile read MUST NOT recreate a missing account. That address alone is not proof of control over another account.
- **FR-009**: Synchronization of a recognized identity MUST NOT erase known information when it is temporarily missing. A usable new provider address may be shared with another principal and is synchronized without merging accounts. The holder retains access to their recognized account, subject to other restrictions; a loading failure follows FR-018. Provider information is retrieved at first creation and explicit sign-in, with targeted retrieval for a missing necessary value; no periodic, every-startup or every-business-request synchronization is required. An email may remain unchanged between explicit logins.
- **FR-010**: Migration business recreation MUST preserve established identity associations, Auth0 account age and RevenueCat mappings before the business reset. It MUST require neither a new provider identity nor a mandatory profile form. Shared email is not an identity conflict. F14 owns transition evidence and explicit reauthentication before first personal access to V2; this does not replace entitlement recovery with a new purchase.

#### Profile and authorizations

- **FR-011**: Personal presentation and contact information in the business profile MUST be limited to username, email and optional photo. First name, last name and phone number MUST NOT be copied into this profile. Identity references, account-age evidence and information needed for entitlements, permissions and deletion requests remain possible according to their F11 purpose; this minimization does not erase the Google/Apple profile.
- **FR-012**: The profile MUST be created automatically with an available, valid, neutrally generated username, without an additional form or using the email as its source. Generation MUST obey the same format and case-insensitive uniqueness rules as manual input. Creation MUST use the approved default avatar without retrieving a provider photo; the holder may customize the profile later.
- **FR-013**: Every username, generated or entered, MUST contain 3 to 32 characters from unaccented letters, digits, dot, hyphen and underscore. Surrounding whitespace is removed before validation; internal spaces are forbidden. Uniqueness ignores letter case and remains guaranteed under concurrency. A refused change preserves the previous profile and indicates how to correct it.
- **FR-014**: The authorized holder MUST be able to change their username and replace or delete their photo. Email is not freely edited in this journey. An unchanged field is retained; signing in again does not replace customization with the provider value. Success is announced only after the outcome is established; an uncertain response allows a reread without a blind modification.
- **FR-015**: The photo MUST remain optional; only PNG or JPEG files of 5 MiB or less are accepted, consistent with the retained V1 capability. Refusal or replacement failure preserves the previous photo. Photo deletion requires explicit intent; omission of the field does not trigger it. Replaced or removed files held by Blockout follow applicable erasure, without deleting the original file at Google/Apple.
- **FR-016**: Private reading, editing and deletion of the account MUST be limited to the holder or the support process explicitly authorized for the relevant purpose. A session or moderation role does not grant access to all accounts or allow self-granting a permission. Profile creation does not reactivate an account whose deletion has been accepted.

#### Sessions and isolation

- **FR-017**: Guest, sign-in in progress, authenticated identity with unavailable profile, usable account, expired/revoked session and deletion in progress MUST have distinct outcomes. Provider success alone does not announce a ready profile; waiting or error states allow exit or retry instead of endless loading.
- **FR-018**: If the identity is authenticated but the profile unavailable, the system MUST preserve the ability to retry without unnecessary reauthentication while the evidence remains valid. Public content remains accessible; personal actions requiring the profile remain suspended. A synchronization outage justifies neither creating a duplicate nor using the previous profile of another identity.
- **FR-019**: A transient network or provider outage MUST NOT be presented as revocation. An invalid or expired access token cannot authorize protected actions; a confirmed inability to renew requires sign-in when valid access is no longer available. The system MUST follow standard Auth0 token validity, without promising that ending a provider session or removing refresh tokens retroactively invalidates every signed access token. Current business-account status and permissions still apply. No write is considered accepted without an established outcome; no offline personal-mutation queue is introduced. The same-subject return limitation is explicit in FR-035.
- **FR-020**: Sign-out MUST remove authentication and local personal information even if browser session closure fails. It allows continuing as a guest without claiming to sign out the global Google/Apple account or other devices. Voluntary deletion, in contrast, blocks the targeted business account on all devices. Neither case promises global Google/Apple logout or nonstandard retroactive access-token invalidation; FR-035 defines the accepted return limit.
- **FR-021**: Account switching or entering guest mode MUST isolate profiles, follows, notifications, permissions and entitlements; late responses and not-yet-accepted actions from the old session MUST NOT populate or modify the new one. A mutation already accepted for A must not be reassigned to B. This mobile/context isolation is distinct from the standard Auth0 access-token limit for same-subject re-registration in FR-035; it does not require a persistent business session registry.
- **FR-022**: Sign-out, deletion and account switching MUST remove or invalidate the old account’s notification association with the relevant destination. Signing out does not invalidate destinations on the same account’s other devices. F07 defines failure recovery and prevention of inappropriate subsequent sends; failure does not count as success and recalling an already delivered notification is not guaranteed.
- **FR-023**: Entitlements MUST belong to the current account and come from RevenueCat SDK state under F09. No available state means no advertising or purchase invitation; an inactive SDK state may allow advertising under F11 consent rules. Public sport remains accessible in every state. No custom refresh/tolerance clock or backend rights projection is added. Guests and new account sessions retain no previous account entitlement.

#### Account-age evidence

- **FR-024**: Account-age evidence MUST come from creation of the retained Auth0 principal, shared by its already linked identities, not from business profile creation. Migration or reinstall does not reset this reference; voluntary deletion followed by a new registration does not recover the erased account age. F08 owns the seven-day threshold and moderation exception. Account age does not allow inferring a person’s age.
- **FR-025**: A known date reliably attributed to the principal MUST remain usable during provider unavailability. Without reliable evidence, the system MUST NOT substitute any arbitrary or business date and MUST allow retry; only actions requiring this evidence are suspended. A moderation exception requires the applicable permission to be established.

#### Deletion and return

- **FR-026**: The holder MUST be able to request deletion in the application after explicit confirmation explaining its consequences, possible ongoing processing and the distinction from store billing. A subscription does not require waiting for expiry; access to its management is provided. Cancellation before confirmation preserves the account. Account ownership must be established before acceptance, with reauthentication if the available evidence no longer allows it.
- **FR-027**: The request MUST be durably accepted before any external erasure operation and before voluntary session closure that would prevent recording it. Definite failure before acceptance keeps the account usable. Upon acceptance, the targeted business account becomes unusable on all devices, the local session is closed and acceptance is confirmed. A lost response requires finding the outcome, not assuming a failure without effects.
- **FR-028**: After acceptance, erasure MUST remain an identifiable pending obligation despite application closure or provider failure. Keep one deletion state and only the original references needed to complete cleanup. Attempt ordinary cleanup automatically after durable acceptance; diagnose and resolve incomplete cleanup manually with existing authorized tools. No persisted per-step status ledger, retry history, periodic recovery, workflow engine, special resume command or new administration screen is required. Do not recreate the account while its cleanup remains unresolved or let old cleanup reactivate it.
- **FR-029**: Deletion MUST cover the business profile, the Auth0 profile and its associations, applicable revocations including Sign in with Apple, the RevenueCat customer record and personal data held by Blockout according to the matrix below. It does not delete the person’s Google/Apple accounts or automatically cancel their store subscriptions. A complete record MUST NOT be retained to facilitate future purchase restoration.
- **FR-030**: Useful live contributions MUST be retainable without association to the deleted account and without changing their moderation state. Their ownership does not automatically return to a recreated account. Personal information contained in the link itself remains subject to applicable F11 requests; removing the association is not a promise of total anonymization. F08 details treatment of report attribution and moderation history under these constraints.
- **FR-031**: Repeated erasure requests and authorized manual continuation MUST resolve the original accepted request and original targets without additional unintended damage or reactivation. Preserve enough minimal evidence to find an incomplete request and inspect what remains using existing tools, without asking the user to submit the whole deletion again or requiring an application record for every step. A repeated deletion intent must still target the original business account, refusing without effect if that account has been replaced. Old cleanup must never target a later account sharing an email or provider reference. No universal generation/attempt protocol or automatic retry mechanism is imposed.
- **FR-032**: The communicated state MUST distinguish acceptance, processing in progress and an established outcome. Provider acceptance does not prove instant physical erasure. Pending operations and any justified retention remain explicit under F11; support is accessible without an account. The resolution criterion MUST rely on actually available guarantees and allow operator handling of blocks, without waiting for evidence the provider does not supply. No new deadline or continuous human processing is promised; applicable F11 deadlines remain respected.
- **FR-033**: Recreation with the same identity MUST wait for resolution of identity and purchase operations that could affect the new account. During this wait, the system offers the deletion-in-progress state, guest access and support. Resolution allows new registration; no delayed retry may erase the new account or restored purchases. Independent legally justified retention does not by itself constitute an indefinite prohibition on recreation.
- **FR-034**: A restored backup or reconstruction MUST respect current deletion, rights and privacy obligations before affected data is made available. Standard provider checks and authorized manual review/correction are permitted under F13; unresolved affected resources remain unavailable. No generic automatic recovery engine or synchronous external evidence store is imposed. Backup retention is not active-profile retention.
- **FR-035**: Registration after deletion MUST create a new account lifecycle without restoring old business data, follows, notifications, personal permissions, account age, contribution attribution or manual Pro benefits. Possible reuse of an address or provider identifier is insufficient to restore these elements; baseline permissions for every new registration remain governed by F12. These ordinary capabilities are automatically assigned to the usable account, subject to business conditions; no administrative or moderation privilege is automatically restored or granted. V2 relies on standard Auth0 access-token validity: if a social principal subject is reused after resolved deletion, an earlier still-valid token may authorize the new account under its current permissions until it expires. No extra re-registration delay, custom lifecycle claim or business session registry is added to prevent this case. This accepted limitation does not permit restoring old business data or applying stale cleanup to the new account.
- **FR-036**: After authentication on a usable account, explicit native restoration MUST use the standard RevenueCat/store transfer scope, including any receipt-wide scope, without a Blockout profile merge or revival of deleted personal data or gifts. Blockout does not guarantee transfer purchase by purchase or preservation of other paid purchases outside the native transfer scope. The sign-in identity and paying store account remain distinct. F09 owns actual provider qualification, uncertainty and verified assistance when native restoration is impossible. Email matching never replaces ownership evidence.
- **FR-037**: A prominent deletion section of the existing public privacy page, reachable by a direct link, MUST identify Blockout and provide the procedure and existing F11 contact email for requesting erasure without reinstalling the application or mandatory sign-in. This complements in-app deletion and satisfies the external request-resource requirement of Google Play. No additional account portal, dedicated web form or separate deletion page is selected. Proportionate ownership verification precedes acceptance; a reply address alone does not authorize deletion. Processing follows the same erasure lifecycle and F11 outcome obligations. F14 qualifies the store-published link.
- **FR-038**: Profile data, ownership evidence, deletion states and associated records MUST remain accessible only to recipients authorized for their purpose. Individual requests and support documents follow [F11 FR-021–FR-037](../004-advertising-privacy-legal/spec.md), without adding periodic purging of GitHub issues or retaining personal data indefinitely by default. No secret, payment evidence or personal identifier may appear in public diagnostics or error messages.
- **FR-039**: F05 journeys MUST apply [F13](../003-shared-quality/spec.md) for quality objectives, mobile accessibility, language, understandable errors, security and degraded states. They distinguish loading, absence, refusal, failure and an uncertain outcome; an unavailable state allows retry or a usable public journey. F13 objectives do not promise the same turnaround for all asynchronous external processing.

### Key entities

| Concept                                       | Meaning and limits                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provider identity                             | Authenticated Google or Apple identity; distinct from a declared address and the paying store account.                                                                                                          |
| Auth0 principal                               | Primary identity reference preserved during migration, with its existing associations and account-age reference.                                                                                                |
| Business account / profile                    | Blockout account and its username, email and photo information; recreating storage for migration does not create a new person.                                                                                  |
| Session                                       | Current access context, limited to one account or guest mode; results from the old context do not belong to the next.                                                                                           |
| Account-age evidence                          | Reliable date attributed to the current account’s principal; distinct from a person’s age and a business creation date.                                                                                         |
| Deletion request                              | Verified intent, durable acceptance, a single pending lifecycle state, necessary original targets and the established outcome; no per-step workflow ledger or targeting of every future reuse of an identifier. |
| Payment customer record                       | RevenueCat references and information needed for paid entitlements; distinct from the business account and store-retained transactions.                                                                         |

## Success criteria _(mandatory)_

### Measurable outcomes

- **SC-001**: All A01–A08 journeys lead to the expected account, an explicit refusal or a usable retry; no new association, merge or duplicate business creation is allowed. Eligible first access requires no additional profile form. (FR-001–FR-010, FR-012)
- **SC-002**: For A09–A12, all accepted usernames meet format and uniqueness rules; no rejected change alters the profile and no failed photo replacement removes the old one. No provider last name, first name or phone number is copied into the business profile. (FR-011–FR-016)
- **SC-003**: In A13–A19, no sign-in or entitlement state is announced without an established outcome; no data, permission or entitlement from A populates B or guest mode. All unavailable states allow retry or public access; no advertising or repurchase invitation appears for undetermined entitlements without usable evidence. (FR-017–FR-023, FR-038–FR-039)
- **SC-004**: In A20–A22, all usable evidence corresponds to the current account’s principal; no migration business creation resets account age, no missing date is invented and no voluntary deletion restores the old account age. (FR-024–FR-025)
- **SC-005**: For every failure point in A23–A26, the request is either unaccepted and without destructive effect, or findable and continued without reactivation. No acceptance response counts as confirmation of total erasure; every incomplete request remains findable and diagnosable for manual resolution without a per-step progress ledger. (FR-026–FR-029, FR-031–FR-032)
- **SC-006**: A27–A29 restore neither a deleted account, removed personal attribution nor visibility of hidden content; no retry of the old erasure changes the new account. (FR-030–FR-035)
- **SC-007**: A30–A31 qualify native restoration separately on both stores, including the actual receipt-wide transfer scope and account isolation. No old business data or deleted gift returns; no purchase-by-purchase transfer guarantee is invented. Unavailable restoration has the F09 assistance direction. (FR-035–FR-036)
- **SC-008**: A32 allows requesting deletion outside the application without reinstalling; every erasure action results from verified ownership and meets F11 outcome obligations. Every matrix category has a purpose and functional owner, without false promises of erasure at a third party. (FR-029, FR-037–FR-038)

## Assumptions and dependencies

- iOS and Android are the selected platforms. Google and Apple through Auth0 are existing product choices; no new provider or technical reference scheme is selected here.
- Profile minimization does not remove clubs’ sporting contact information, which belongs to F02/F11. Profile email remains distinct from the freely editable reply address of an F10 report.
- F09 owns native SDK entitlement state, restoration evidence, manual-assignment permissions and handling of independent entitlements. F05 imposes identity, deletion and isolation boundaries without setting those parameters in its place.
- F08 retains the seven-day threshold and other publication rules; available account-age evidence alone does not guarantee eligibility. F12 assigns permissions without reintroducing identity linking or manual-benefit restoration through support.
- F13 applies to degraded states and their operational visibility, without guaranteed human on-call coverage. Response deadlines for data requests belong to F11. Use standard provider behavior and proportionate manual handling to complete outstanding obligations; no generic recovery platform is required and failures cannot silently abandon an accepted erasure.
- F14 qualifies historical identities and purchases, the dependency on role assignment, stopping V1 mutations and conditions before reset. No email ownership reservation is carried across the business reset.
- [Design references](../../docs/design.md#review-references) retain the global handoff and 2026-10-06 scoped approval for guest/sign-in access, shared emails, unavailable profiles, failed editing, pending entitlements and deletion/return. Material changes need revalidation; the existing approval does not qualify native Auth0/RevenueCat behavior or accept unrelated technical dossiers.

## Owner clarifications — 2026-10-06

- Use standard Auth0 SDK/provider behavior and Spring JWT validation. Do not build a business session registry, connection flag, per-device session list, custom token claim, refresh loop or re-registration delay. The same-subject, still-valid-access-token limitation is accepted explicitly in FR-035; mobile cache isolation and stale-cleanup protections remain unchanged.

- Keep in-app deletion and add the Google Play external request route as a prominent section of the existing privacy page with the F11 contact email, without a new form or portal.
