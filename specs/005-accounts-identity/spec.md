## User Scenarios & Testing

The actors are the visitor, the authenticated person, the account holder and authorized personnel handling a support request or failed external cleanup. Possession of an email address or a claimed identifier does not prove account ownership. A moderation permission grants neither access to another person’s profile nor manual Pro benefits. F12 assigns permissions; each personal action remains limited to its owner.

The scenarios define expected behavior; [quickstart.md](quickstart.md) supplies runnable validation cases. A, FR and SC references are local to F05. Qualification accounts and evidence remain private, without personal identifiers in public deliverables.

### User Story 1 — Access the product and recover my account (Priority: P1)

As a visitor or account holder, I want to browse public content and recover my account using my usual method, without a mandatory profile form or unexpected linking.

**Why this priority**: Identity determines personal actions and entitlement continuity; a sign-in failure must not close public content or support.

**Independent Test**: Exercise first access, both selected providers, an already linked identity, two principals sharing an address and missing provider information, with and without an existing business profile.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A01** — **Given** an installation with no signed-in account, **when** first entry follows the completed or skipped discovery, **then** ordinary Login offers its explicit guest action, legal documents and support. On narrow or short phones, including enlarged text, its complete visible lettering-and-athlete group remains centered in the usable hero on both axes, with matching visible BLOCK/OUT edges, visible horizontal breathing room around the lettering and reachable actions/footer. The decorative athlete may extend slightly beyond a lateral hero edge and be cropped to leave more room for the title. Choosing guest access opens permitted public content; no personal action or Pro benefit from the previous account is granted. An account-only action offers sign-in without being implicitly executed. F12 global gates suppress in-app legal, support and sign-in entries, including direct routes. Maintenance administration requires an already signed-in usable operator; a disconnected or expired operator cannot reconnect from that gate. (FR-001–FR-003)
2. **A02** — **Given** onboarding is presented, **when** the person completes it, skips it or refuses notifications, **then** they can continue. V2 completion is retained on the installation and is not reset by sign-out or business-profile recreation. F14 presents this common discovery once on first V2 use, including installations that completed V1 onboarding, without a separate migration welcome. (FR-002)
3. **A03** — **Given** a first Google or Apple sign-in and a usable provider address, **when** the identity is authenticated, **then** a single usable profile is created automatically, with a neutral valid username and the default avatar, without retrieving a provider photo; no mandatory additional form is requested. Two concurrent retries for the same identity find the same account. (FR-003, FR-004, FR-012, FR-013)
4. **A04** — **Given** an Auth0 principal with Google and Apple identities already linked, even with different addresses, **when** the person signs in with each, before or after business profile recreation, **then** they find the same account and its applicable entitlements under the F09/F14 restoration and continuity rules. A secondary identity or historical purchase identifier does not constitute a new business account. On transition to V2, explicit F14 reauthentication preserves this continuity. (FR-005, FR-010, FR-023)
5. **A05** — **Given** two distinct, unlinked Auth0 principals supplying the same usable email, **when** each explicitly establishes its profile, **then** two distinct business accounts are allowed, including case differences in the email. Neither profile is merged or disclosed to the other. Concurrent creation for one principal still produces only one account. (FR-004, FR-006, FR-007)
6. **A06** — **Given** two unlinked identities using distinct addresses, including an Apple private address, **when** they are used, **then** no personal equivalence is inferred; each eligible account remains distinct. Two populated accounts are neither merged nor emptied by a support request or purchase restoration. (FR-004, FR-006, FR-007, FR-036)
7. **A07** — **Given** first creation without a usable address or with failed provider retrieval, **when** the explicit connection process reaches profile creation, **then** no account is announced as ready, no address is fabricated and no additional verification form is shown. Ordinary Login remains with “Se connecter”, the short standard failure toast, support and explicit guest access; a later connection is another explicit process, without an authentication Retry state or automatic replay. (FR-003, FR-008, FR-017, FR-018)
8. **A08** — **Given** a recognized identity with an existing profile, **when** provider email is temporarily missing, **then** the reliable stored value remains. A usable updated email may also belong to another principal: it is synchronized without merging accounts. Loading failure remains an unavailable state. (FR-007–FR-009, FR-018)

### User Story 2 — Use and customize a minimal profile (Priority: P2)

As an account holder, I want a useful profile without unnecessarily copying my personal information, and to change my username or photo without loss on failure.

**Why this priority**: Optional profile customization follows usable account access. Minimal automatic profile creation and privacy remain prerequisites under User Story 1.

**Independent Test**: Create a profile, edit its fields separately, trigger a username conflict and a photo failure, then sign in again with the identity.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A09** — **Given** a provider also returning last name, first name and phone number, **when** the business profile is created or synchronized, **then** only username, email and photo constitute its personal presentation and contact information. References required for identity, entitlements and lifecycle remain separate; no deletion of the Google/Apple profile is inferred from this minimization. (FR-011, FR-012)
2. **A10** — **Given** username candidates that are too short, too long, contain forbidden characters or are already used, **when** a username is generated, **then** the result follows the same rules as manual input. When two people simultaneously request the same username regardless of letter case, at most one obtains it; the other keeps their old username and can correct it, or explicitly retries creation through its ordinary connection/establishment action. (FR-012, FR-013)
3. **A11** — **Given** a customized profile, **when** the holder changes only their username and then signs in again, **then** the photo and customized username are preserved except for the requested change. An invalid or conflicting username rejects the atomic save and identifies the username field without losing other input. An attempt to edit, privately read or delete another person’s account is refused. (FR-004, FR-013, FR-014, FR-016)
4. **A12** — **Given** an old photo, **when** replacement fails or the file is rejected, **then** the previous photo remains available and the same-account draft survives. Selection includes square crop and passive in-field display; cancelling preserves the previous choice, and a source requiring adaptation is checked against upload rules after preparation. Image validation, including an excessive payload or unsupported media type, identifies the photo field. Successful explicit deletion removes the photo; a missing photo does not prevent account use. A failed or lost-response save keeps the same mounted input and prepared photo directly editable, with ordinary failure feedback and a later explicit manual submission. No verification read, separate uncertainty card or return-to-edit mode is required, and no recovery automatically replays the save. (FR-014, FR-015)
5. **A33** — **Given** a usable signed-in account, **when** its holder opens the Profile tab, **then** “Mon profil” presents the accepted identity and grouped entries without editable fields or a save action. The overview retains the permitted main navigation. Its edit action opens the separate “Modifier mon profil” screen without the main bar or Pro offer. An accepted save returns to the overview with the confirmed identity; a following account-read failure presents failed reading without reverting that confirmed save. Cancellation/Back/system Back returns without saving and applies the existing dirty-draft discard confirmation. Refusing discard keeps the same editor, focus and input. Neither cancellation nor back signs out. (FR-014, FR-020, FR-040)
6. **A34** — **Given** guest access, an unavailable account read, uncertain Pro state or a session change during profile navigation, **when** the overview or editor is displayed, **then** the active first account read uses the page-shaped skeleton under [F13 FR-038](../003-shared-quality/spec.md#functional-requirements); disabled, idle and suspended observations do not simulate active waiting. The authorized overview and editor are independent of optional photo loading/failure, which stays in the shared media slot and cannot hide identity, entries or form actions. Once native identity is resolved under FR-017, account loading or recovery stays in the dependent body while ordinary navigation remains available. Initial and later final required-read failures replace that body with the common card while authorized account data, the mounted same-account draft and prepared photo remain in their existing owners and stable native tree. Safe Retry rereads the account, keeps the card and indicates only its active attempt; successful recovery restores the same draft. A current account/session refusal withdraws inaccessible data and commands without a render exception; a later technical failure cannot restore them. An absent editor account and direct guest editor entry use the same sign-in presentation with contextual copy. Existing illustrated invitation cards follow FR-003’s lettering and complete-group centering while retaining intentional athlete overflow and readable messages/actions. A different principal or business UUID opens its own context. Public navigation remains reachable outside F12 blocking, Pro never gates account readiness, and sign-out retains its explicitly named session action. (FR-003, FR-016–FR-023, FR-039, FR-040)

### User Story 3 — Control my session without mixing accounts (Priority: P1)

As a user, I want to recover after an outage, sign out and switch accounts without exposing my data or assigning my entitlements to someone else.

**Why this priority**: The sign-in outcome, business data loading and entitlement establishment are distinct; confusing them exposes information or unnecessarily blocks access.

**Independent Test**: Vary authentication, profile and entitlement loading, then switch accounts with delayed responses and a failed provider session closure.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A13** — **Given** a cancelled, failed or successful explicit connection process, **when** the person returns, **then** successful Auth0 authentication establishes the native identity, and success of the following explicit account-establishment step completes the process and enters the application. The same “Se connecter” entry opens Google/Apple in production and email/password with native signup in preproduction. An Auth0 or establishment failure keeps ordinary Login, its normal copy and the same “Se connecter” action with only the short standard top toast; the actual SDK identity is retained, with no separate authentication Retry or automatic replay. The independent account read then follows A14. The database connection cannot authenticate the production application; preproduction cannot delete a social or cross-connection principal. Guest access and support remain available outside global gates. (FR-003, FR-008, FR-017, FR-038, FR-039)
2. **A14** — **Given** a usable restored native identity whose Blockout account has not yet loaded, **when** the native splash gives way to ordinary entry and the first account read is active or finally fails, **then** the permitted application navigation and exact requested destination are already open, without an intermediate React bootstrap screen. Home uses its existing calendar skeleton; a cold/direct sporting link retains its destination and Search selection. Private reads and commands wait for a usable matching business account. A final technical account-read failure replaces only its dependent personal content with the common card; safe Retry keeps that card and the route, with progress only for the explicit active read. It never starts hosted login, clears credentials, creates an account, replays a command or invents Empty. Canonical account absence alone offers explicit establishment; ambiguous backend refusal is not SDK evidence of nonrenewable credentials. Public version/maintenance gates retain precedence, and no prior account data is borrowed. (FR-001, FR-008, FR-017–FR-021)
3. **A15** — **Given** an established session, **when** a network outage occurs, **then** it is not treated as revocation and no personal write is announced as accepted without confirmation. **When** nonrenewable expiry or revocation is confirmed, **then** protected actions require reauthentication without preventing public consultation. (FR-019, FR-039)
4. **A16** — **Given** a signed-in account, **when** browser logout fails or is cancelled, **then** the application reports the failure and retains the actual authenticated SDK state for retry. Successful logout or an explicit local credential-clear action enters guest mode and retires its private component/cache lifetime. Neither action claims global Google/Apple or other-device logout. (FR-020, FR-021, FR-023)
5. **A17** — **Given** account A with profile, follow, notification or entitlement requests in progress, **when** the person switches to account B or guest mode and A’s responses then arrive, **then** none populates the new session. A personal operation not yet accepted does not execute under B. (FR-021, FR-023)
6. **A18** — **Given** a device associated with A, **when** sign-out disassociation fails, **then** local sign-out still completes without a persistent retry. Old-account system pushes may continue until the association is corrected, but cannot expose A’s private data in guest/B context; an old removal cannot remove B’s association. Erasure removes A’s destinations server-side. No notification counter or count-only account state is established; any previous app-controlled native badge is cleared nonblockingly under F07. (FR-022)
7. **A19** — **Given** a signed-in account without usable entitlement evidence, **when** verification fails, **then** all public sporting consultation, including pool maps and full club calendars, remains accessible without advertising or an invitation to repurchase. Retry is possible and waiting never establishes a free or active Pro status. Account switching preserves isolation under FR-023. (FR-023)

### User Story 5 — Delete my account and return without recovering my old data (Priority: P1)

As an account holder, I want to delete my account, return directly to guest access and later recover a valid purchase without reactivating my old data.

**Why this priority**: Blockout must control removal of its business data while keeping external cleanup and operator handling proportionate.

**Independent Test**: Delete with and without a subscription; simulate a local failure, a lost response and provider failures; verify guest return, manual diagnostics, fresh business registration and native purchase restoration on each store.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **A23** — **Given** an authenticated holder, possibly subscribed, **when** they open deletion, **then** an ordinary confirmation screen opened from the profile, without the main navigation bar or Pro offer, briefly explains irreversible personal-data removal and that any Pro subscription must be managed in its App Store or Google Play. There is no subscription-management action in this screen; a checkbox and explicit deletion action confirm intent. Cancelling preserves the account. Confirmation precedes deletion; sign-out must not prevent the authenticated operation. (FR-026, FR-027)
2. **A24** — **Given** an explicitly confirmed deletion intent, **when** the local transaction definitively fails, **then** no external deletion starts and the account and session remain usable. A failed or lost response uses ordinary mutation failure feedback, retains confirmation input and permits an explicit manual new send to the original target without a verification read or dedicated recovery mode. An acknowledged deletion uses standard local sign-out and returns to guest home. (FR-027, FR-031)
3. **A25** — **Given** committed business deletion, **when** an external cleanup fails, **then** local deletion remains effective, other known providers are still attempted and the user returns directly to guest home. A private diagnostic supports manual repair; no user-facing progress screen or success toast is added. A process interruption before logging may leave external cleanup unreported. (FR-027–FR-029, FR-031, FR-032)
4. **A26** — **Given** a logged external failure, **when** an authorized operator handles it, **then** they verify the original targets and current situation with existing tools before a destructive action. No business rollback, scheduled retry, deletion ledger or automatic replay is performed. A repeated request for the absent original business account has no provider effects. (FR-028, FR-031, FR-038)
5. **A27** — **Given** a deleted account that published a visible link and a hidden link, **when** its data is processed, **then** useful links may remain without association to the account; the hidden link remains hidden. A recreated account does not recover their ownership. Personal data in the content itself falls under an applicable F11 request, without a promise of total anonymization. (FR-030, FR-035, FR-038)
6. **A28** — **Given** business deletion and a successful provider acknowledgment, **when** the person signs in again explicitly, **then** a new business account may be created without polling for physical provider erasure. External cleanup failure also creates no pending-account block. Concurrent return during an in-flight provider deletion has no special coordination; ordinary sign-in errors and manual assistance apply. (FR-032, FR-033)
7. **A29** — **Given** a new business account, **when** a request still names the deleted original business account, **then** it cannot target the replacement account. Manual external cleanup verifies its original references and current use; reusing retained data reapplies current restrictions before affected data becomes accessible. No broader guarantee for concurrent provider deletion and return is inferred. (FR-031, FR-033–FR-035)
8. **A30** — **Given** a deleted business account and a still-valid store subscription, **when** the person creates a new account then restores the purchase with the store account that paid, **then** paid entitlements can be restored without the old profile, follows, contribution attribution or manual benefit. The Google/Apple identity used to sign in to Blockout need not be the one used by the store. Apple and Google journeys are qualified separately. (FR-029, FR-035, FR-036)
9. **A31** — **Given** a purchase associated with another Blockout account, **when** native restoration is explicitly requested, **then** the standard RevenueCat/store transfer scope applies, potentially to the full receipt, without merging Blockout profiles. Qualification establishes actual effects on both accounts. Cross-store impossibility leads to verified F09 assistance, never assignment by email. (FR-036)
10. **A32** — **Given** a person without the application installed or no longer able to sign in, **when** they follow the public deletion link, **then** they find a procedure and contact for requesting erasure without reinstalling the application. Proportionate ownership verification precedes action on the account; the reply address alone does not authorize deletion. Processing provides the status or result under F11. (FR-037, FR-038)

### Edge Cases

- Email never identifies or reserves an account. A missing business profile during migration does not invalidate established identity associations. (A04–A08)
- The same identity and username may be submitted repeatedly or concurrently without duplicate creation. Deletion targets the original business account; absence never redirects cleanup to its replacement. (A03, A10, A24–A26)
- Successful authentication, a ready profile and established Pro entitlements are three distinct outcomes; their responses may arrive after sign-out. (A14–A19)
- A missing deletion response may follow committed business removal; a definite local failure and an unknown outcome are not equivalent. (A24)
- A documented provider acknowledgment is sufficient even when external processing is asynchronous. No polling or pending-state gate is added; failures use private diagnostics and manual handling, with an accepted interruption-before-log gap. (A25–A29)
- A surviving Auth0 principal restores neither business data, contribution ownership nor manual benefits. (A27, A29–A30)

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

Advertising age declaration and correction belong to [F11](../004-advertising-privacy-legal/spec.md#entitlements-and-advertising-choices). They do not add a profile field, account age gate or identity check. Guest and signed-in users use the same installation-local advertising controls outside F12 blocking gates. Account UUID arrival or session replacement updates current advertising eligibility and discards prepared ads under F11; it does not restart unchanged installation age/consent preparation or persist consent with the account.

#### Access and identity resolution

- **FR-001**: The system MUST allow guest access to public content, legal documents and support before sign-in or after failure. Personal actions require a usable identity and account; their sign-in prompt MUST NOT automatically execute a purchase, publication, deletion or other mutation. F12 global maintenance, unavailable-access and mandatory-update gates take precedence over these ordinary journeys: no in-app legal/support/sign-in entry or direct-route bypass is available. Maintenance administration is limited to an already signed-in usable operator with current permissions; the gate offers no authentication entry. Ordinary sign-in failures outside global blocking retain support and legal access.
- **FR-002**: Onboarding MUST be completable or skippable without mandatory account creation or notification permission. Its V2 completion is installation-specific and retained on sign-out and business-profile recreation. Its content presents only capabilities retained by the specs, including F06 follows and actual F01 coverage, without promising unsupported club following. F14 owns first V2 initialization and the common discovery presented on every installation, including a completed V1 installation; no separate transition welcome or V1-origin detection is added.
- **FR-003**: Production sign-in MUST use Google and Apple through Auth0. Both variants use one “Se connecter” entry opening hosted Universal Login in the platform authentication browser, followed by the existing explicit account-establishment request; Blockout provides no in-app credential or social-login form. Process success enters the application independently of the subsequent account read. An Auth0 or establishment failure MUST keep ordinary Login, its normal copy and the same “Se connecter” action with only the short standard top toast “Une erreur est survenue.” A later connection is another explicit process, without a dedicated authentication Retry state or automatic replay. The illustrated Login MUST fit the phone viewport without scrolling: its decorative composition adapts proportionally to both the usable width and remaining hero height while the connection actions, compact legal/help footer, touch targets and safe areas remain usable at enlarged text sizes. The visible left and right edges of BLOCK and OUT MUST align exactly as one lettering block, with visible horizontal breathing room around the lettering. On Login, the decorative athlete MAY extend slightly beyond a lateral hero edge and be cropped to leave more room for the title. Center the complete visible lettering-and-athlete group horizontally and vertically in its usable illustration area, accounting for transparent source margins and the athlete’s extent. Existing account invitation cards MUST follow these composition principles while preserving their distinct proportions, separate athlete, intentional overflow beyond the hero frame, rounded outer-card clipping and readable messages/actions. Login’s existing club rail moves continuously left to right and stays static under reduced motion. Preproduction offers only email/password with native public signup for anyone able to use that journey; no manual provisioning, separate test-account button or Blockout password form is required. Its dedicated database connection MUST be disabled in production, and social methods disabled for its application. Retain the existing tenant and distinct application registrations; their shared directory is not tenant isolation. Preproduction deletion MUST NOT target a production social principal or a principal linked to another connection. Cancellation, failure and success remain distinct. The owner qualifies Google/Apple using their own accounts in the production variant; preproduction success does not qualify these native journeys.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST resolve the account from the authenticated identity and its established associations, never from a merely declared email or identifier. Concurrent retries and creations for the same identity MUST lead to a single business account. Actions on an account MUST verify its holder or a permission dedicated to their purpose.
- **FR-005**: Already established Google/Apple associations MUST be preserved, even if their addresses differ. Linked methods find the same business account and its applicable entitlements, independently of a secondary identity or historical payment reference.
- **FR-006**: The system MUST NOT create any new Google/Apple association, even with two authenticated identities or the same address. It MUST neither merge nor delete data from two populated accounts to resolve a conflict. Support follows this rule; recovery of a paid entitlement remains a separate operation.
- **FR-007**: Distinct, unlinked Auth0 principals MAY use the same email, including the same address with different letter case. Each principal resolves its own business account. No email uniqueness reservation, conflict refusal, automatic linking or merge is permitted; email alone never proves ownership.
- **FR-008**: First creation MUST have a usable address from the authenticated identity provider; a missing, unusable or unretrieved address prevents successful creation. Its ordinary connection/establishment action remains available with support and explicit guest access, without fabricating an address or adding an input/verification form. A failed explicit connection process follows FR-003 with the same “Se connecter” action, rather than a separate authentication Retry. Creation occurs only through explicit establishment after authentication. Confirmed canonical account absence offers an explicit creation action and reuses an available authenticated identity. An ordinary profile read or its Retry MUST NOT create an account, start hosted login or synchronize provider information. An ambiguous backend status never authorizes absence-specific creation. The provider address alone is not proof of control over another account.
- **FR-009**: Synchronization of a recognized identity MUST NOT erase known information when it is temporarily missing. A usable new provider address may be shared with another principal and is synchronized without merging accounts. The holder retains access to their recognized account, subject to other restrictions; a loading failure follows FR-018. Provider information is retrieved at first creation and explicit sign-in, with targeted retrieval for a missing necessary value; no periodic, every-startup or every-business-request synchronization is required. An email may remain unchanged between explicit logins.
- **FR-010**: Migration business recreation MUST preserve Auth0 principals and existing associations, without an additional profile form. Each V2 account receives a new business identity used for its RevenueCat association. Valid V1 paid entitlements are recovered through explicit native restoration, with verified manual assistance for exceptions, including unavailable original-store access; no migration engine is required. Necessary historical references remain discoverable for continuity and scoped erasure under F14, without exporting full profiles or merging by email. Gifts remain distinct from store-restorable purchases. F14 owns transition evidence and explicit first V2 reauthentication.

#### Profile and authorizations

- **FR-011**: Personal presentation and contact information in the business profile MUST be limited to username, email and optional photo. First name, last name and phone number MUST NOT be copied into this profile. Identity references and information needed for entitlements, permissions and deletion requests remain possible according to their F11 purpose; this minimization does not erase the Google/Apple profile.
- **FR-012**: Explicit account establishment MUST create the profile with an available, valid, neutrally generated username, without an additional form or using email or provider personal presentation as its source. Generation MUST obey the same format and case-insensitive uniqueness rules as manual input. A rejected generated name uses ordinary connection/establishment failure feedback and a later explicit action under FR-003/FR-008, without automatic replacement candidates or an additional registration form. Creation MUST use the approved default avatar without retrieving a provider photo; the holder may customize the profile later.
- **FR-013**: Every username, generated or entered, MUST contain 3 to 32 characters from unaccented letters, digits, dot, hyphen and underscore. Surrounding whitespace is removed before validation; internal spaces are forbidden. Uniqueness ignores letter case and remains guaranteed under concurrency. A refused change preserves the previous profile and indicates how to correct it.
- **FR-014**: The authorized holder MUST be able to change their username and replace or delete their photo. Email is not freely edited in this journey. The single save applies requested fields together or preserves the previous profile on refusal. Omitted fields retain current state, including concurrent changes; signing in again does not replace customization with provider values. Exact username/image validation identifies the field to correct and preserves other input. Success requires a confirmed response and its confirmed values remain acquired if a following read fails. A failed or lost response uses ordinary mutation failure feedback and preserves the original owner’s mounted input and prepared photo for another explicit manual submission. ## V1 evidence and limitations

The [transition owner](../014-v1-v2-transition/spec.md#v1-evidence) retains historical continuity and qualification limits. The historical functional reference does not establish installed binaries or complete deployed configuration, and authorizes no new repository or provider inspection.

An authorized read-only inspection of the production Auth0 native application on **2026-09-15**, retained here as a `blockout-legacy` observation, found Google and Apple enabled, username/password disabled, refresh-token rotation enabled with a 30-day maximum and idle lifetime disabled. A Google-primary sample had Google and Apple identities. These samples establish neither population completeness nor all credential-invalidation paths. No production linking, deletion or login-continuity qualification was performed. [F14](../014-v1-v2-transition/spec.md#v1-evidence) owns the observed post-login dependency; [F09](../006-pro-subscriptions/spec.md#v1-evidence-and-limitations) owns payment/provider observations. The former V1 architecture described intended ownership, not proof of implemented entitlement or authorization boundaries.

The current requirements retain principal-based resolution without new email linking, minimal profile fields, photo preservation on failure and isolated session/deletion outcomes. External deletion is not a distributed transaction: local rollback cannot undo provider erasure. [Qualification](quickstart.md) separates controlled local behavior from provider, store and physical-device results.

Public guidance supplies provider constraints, not evidence of Blockout qualification:

- [RevenueCat — deleting a customer](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/dashboard-and-metrics/customer-profile#delete-customer) distinguishes the customer record from the store subscription; its [deletion interface](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/api-v1/customers#delete-customer) indicates asynchronous processing. - [Apple — in-app deletion](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/) describes, among other things, Sign in with Apple revocation and subscription information; [Google Play — deletion request](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en) also requires a path accessible outside the application. FR-026–FR-037 express the retained outcomes without certifying current store declarations.
